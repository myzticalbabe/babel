# STEM Learning Standards — The Six Research-Backed Practices

> **Source:** Soul Forge License + NGCP Brite Practices (2026) + 30by30 K-12 Guide + CREST Awards + Springer 2026

---

## The Six Practices

These six practices are the current consensus set — belonging and relevance, investigation, normalized struggle, stereotype challenge, collaborative work, diverse role models. Every curriculum artifact in the BaBeL Canon maps to at least one.

---

## 1. Belonging and Relevance

> **"STEM is for me, and it connects to my world."**

### What it means
Learners see themselves in STEM and understand how it connects to their lives, communities, and interests.

### Implementation
- **Community-building icebreakers** (Brite Practice 1): "Technology Babies" — *"Think back to your first memory using digital technology. How old were you? What did you use? What else do you remember?"*
- **Identity mapping** — Learners map STEM connections to their hobbies, culture, family
- **Local problem framing** — Every challenge starts with a real problem in the learner's community
- **Family engagement** — Materials include home-connection activities in EN/ES minimum

### Assessment
- Pre/post "I belong in STEM" Likert scale
- Learner-generated problem statements
- Family participation rates

---

## 2. Investigation

> **"I ask questions and design ways to answer them."**

### What it means
Learners engage in authentic scientific/engineering practices — not step-following, but genuine inquiry.

### Implementation
- **Driving questions** — Every unit starts with a question learners help shape
- **Design cycles** — Plan → Build → Test → Reflect → Iterate (non-linear)
- **Failure normalized** — "Failed prototype" is data, not defeat
- **Evidence-based argumentation** — Learners defend designs with data

### Assessment
- Science/Engineering practice rubrics (NGSS SEPs)
- Design portfolio with iteration evidence
- Peer critique quality

---

## 3. Normalized Struggle

> **"Getting stuck is part of the work. I know how to get unstuck."**

### What it means
Struggle is reframed as necessary for learning. Learners develop metacognitive strategies.

### Implementation
- **Struggle protocols** — "Three before me" (self → peer → resource → facilitator)
- **Debugging journals** — Learners document: stuck point → strategy tried → result
- **Growth reflection** — Weekly: "What was hard? What did I try? What worked?"
- **Facilitator modeling** — Adults share their own stuck moments

### Assessment
- Strategy repertoire checklist
- Persistence metrics (time-on-task, retry count)
- Self-efficacy surveys

---

## 4. Stereotype Challenge

> **"STEM people look like me, live like me, and come from everywhere."**

### What it means
Actively counter narrow stereotypes about who does STEM and what STEM looks like.

### Implementation
- **Diverse role models** (Brite Practice 3): 6+ real scientists with varied identities, hobbies, pathways
- **Hobby integration** — Role models share non-STEM passions
- **Pathway diversity** — Non-linear careers, late starts, career switches highlighted
- **IF/THEN® Collection** — Free digital resources for counter-stereotype images

### Assessment
- "Draw a Scientist" pre/post analysis
- Career interest breadth
- Identity alignment surveys

---

## 5. Collaborative Work

> **"We build better together. My team needs what I bring."**

### What it means
Learning is social. Learners develop teamwork, communication, and shared ownership.

### Implementation
- **Structured team roles** — Rotating: facilitator, recorder, materials, questioner
- **Pair programming / buddy building** — Mandatory for complex tasks
- **Gallery walks** — Learners give/receive feedback on works-in-progress
- **Project showcases** (Brite Practice 6) — Live demos + digital gallery with comments/likes
- **Family/community presentations** — Authentic audiences

### Assessment
- Collaboration rubric (communication, equity, shared ownership)
- Peer feedback quality
- Team artifact coherence

---

## 6. Diverse Role Models

> **"I see my future self in the people I meet."**

### What it means
Sustained exposure to diverse STEM professionals — not one-off "guest speakers."

### Implementation
- **Role model stories** — Early life, hobbies, challenges, perseverance (Brite Practice 4)
- **Interactive formats** — "Would You Rather: STEM Jobs Edition" with volcanologist Jess Phoenix
- **Mentorship matching** (Springer 2026): Online mentoring + in-school STEAM clubs = highest impact
  - Online: Female STEM experts, identity-affirming engagement
  - In-school: Teacher-led clubs, practical application
  - Bridge: Shared projects, common language, facilitator coordination
- **STEM Champion Training** (NGCP) — Free certification for mentors

### Evidence Base
| Source | Key Finding |
|--------|-------------|
| NGCP Brite Practices (2026) | 6 practices → engagement + persistence gains |
| 30 by 30 K-12 Guide | Interactivity levels 0-4 → level 4 = full engagement + mentorship |
| CREST Awards (BSA) | Role models + active learning + inclusive culture = long-term engagement |
| Springer 2026 (Dahn et al.) | Online mentoring + in-school clubs > either alone; bridging quality matters |

## Assessment Philosophy

**Mastery-based, portfolio-driven, identity-inclusive.**

| Type | Purpose | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Diagnostic** | Pre-unit interests, misconceptions | Concept cartoon + interview |
| **Formative** | Daily struggle journal + peer critique | Debugging journal entry |
| **Summative** | Design portfolio + presentation | Circuit design + community demo |
| **Identity** | "I am a STEM person" tracking | Annual survey + artifacts |

### No Grades
Soul Forge License: "The soul knows its smith." Assessment serves the learner's growth, not sorting.
